# Script and story: optional reference

**Not a required method.** The voice rules in `SKILL.md` are mandatory. Everything below is only what happened to work on one deck, for when the user asks for help with structure.

## What worked

- One thesis for the whole talk.
- Three acts.
- "Why am I hearing this now?" asked of every section.
- The story creates the conclusion. No new evidence in the final minute.
- Don't explain right after a strong line. Let it sit.
- About 1400 words for 10 minutes. A shorter version was offered and refused, so don't assume shorter is wanted.
- Aware of the rubric without saying so.

## Slides and script together

- The script is the authority. Slides don't add claims it doesn't make.
- When the speaker trims a line, check whether the slide now has to carry it. If they stop naming something aloud, a caption may become load-bearing. If they stop describing a screenshot, the crop has to keep the part that explains it. Name such consequences. Don't argue the trim.
- Notes in `notes.txt` match beats: one block per state where the words change.

## Voice in practice

These came from corrections.

- Meaning is framed around other people, not around how the speaker felt.
- Boastful labels were removed.
- A catchy line that misled was replaced by a plainer, accurate one.
- Each lesson must be a different lesson, named exactly.

Check spoken text with `references/wording.md`, then read it aloud against the speaker's sample. If it could appear on LinkedIn, rewrite it.
